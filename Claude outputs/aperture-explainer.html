Field guide

Aperture, explained from the ground up

A cold outreach product where the customer types one thing, their website address, and everything else is set up for them. This page explains what it does, how the onboarding works, what happens inside the analysis, how the data reaches Apollo, what is finished and what is not.

01

What we are building

Cold email works, but setting it up is miserable. To do it properly a business owner has to buy a lead list, buy extra domains so their main domain does not get burned, configure DNS records they do not understand, create mailboxes, warm those mailboxes for two or three weeks, write the emails, connect a sending tool, and then watch bounces and replies every day. That is roughly two weeks of work and four subscriptions before a single email goes out.

Aperture replaces all of that with one input. The customer types their website address. We read the site, work out what they sell and who buys it, show them how many of those buyers exist and what we would send, and once they pay we handle the domains, the inboxes, the warmup, the lead sourcing, the verification, the sending and the replies.

02

The onboarding, step by step

The domain is typed on the landing page. Everything after that happens inside one flow, and the state lives on an Onboarding row in the database, so the customer can close the tab and come back to the same place.

Create the account working

Email and password, or Google. Registration creates the user, the organization and the owner membership in one transaction. The analysis is already running in the background while they type.

POST /auth/register · /auth/oauth/google/start · POST /onboarding/start

Business analysis working

We read the homepage and up to nine common subpages, then ask the model for facts about the business with the page each fact came from, plus two or three buyer groups. Facts are editable in place. Groups can be switched on and off, and the target audience is the union of the ones left on. If the site cannot be read, three questions build the same thing.

POST /onboarding/analysis · POST /onboarding/answers

Free preview working

How many people match, how many have a verified email, a sample of real prospects, and the first emails we would send. No card yet, nothing sent.

GET /onboarding/market · POST /onboarding/preview

Daily volume working

They choose how many emails a day. From that we work out how many inboxes and domains are needed, using forty sends per warm inbox. The recommended pace is the one that leaves at least three months of new people in their market.

GET /billing/plans · Plan table: Launch, Growth, Scale

Sending domains partly working

We generate lookalike domain ideas from their brand and check DNS to flag the ones already registered. They pick. Buying them still needs a registrar integration, so chosen domains are stored as pending registration and the screen says exactly that.

GET /onboarding/domains/ideas · PUT /onboarding/domains

Inboxes and warmup partly working

Inboxes per domain, up to three sender names rotated across addresses, and the warmup timeline: fourteen days by default, first emails at ten to fifteen per inbox, full volume after the ramp. A faster start on pre-warmed inboxes is priced separately. Creating the actual Google or Microsoft inboxes needs a provider we have not integrated, so real sending currently uses mailboxes already connected in Smartlead.

Mailbox rows per domain · Settings → Sending attaches real Smartlead mailboxes

Payment working

Stripe subscription created as incomplete, card confirmed with Stripe's own payment element so no card data touches our servers. Subscription, payment and invoice rows are written from Stripe webhooks, never from the browser.

POST /billing/checkout · webhook → Subscription, Payment, Invoice

Campaign start working

The first campaign is built from the analysis and provisioned in Smartlead: campaign, sequence, schedule, settings, mailboxes, webhook, then start. If something is missing, such as a Smartlead key or an attached mailbox, the screen names the exact prerequisite instead of failing quietly. A CSV of the customer's own contacts can be imported here too.

POST /onboarding/launch

Why this order. Everything free comes first. The customer sees their market, their prospects and their emails before they are asked for money, and everything that costs us real money happens only after the Stripe webhook confirms payment.

03

Inside the business analysis

This is the piece the whole product rests on. Apollo can only be searched with exact values: job titles, seniority levels, company sizes, countries, industry keywords. A business owner cannot produce those, and a form asking for them would empty the page. Their website, though, already contains the answer.

So the analysis has one job: turn a website into a search a machine can run, accurately enough that the customer only has to confirm it.

1 · Fetchhomepage plus up to 9 subpages
→
2 · Extractcode finds country, currency, logos, testimonial titles
→
3 · Modelfacts with sources, plus buyer groups
→
4 · Repaircode fixes values into Apollo's format
→
5 · Sizefree Apollo counts and samples

What comes out of it

Three buckets, and every field in them exists because something downstream reads it.

BucketWhat it holdsWho reads it
Brand detailCompany name, one-liner, what they sell, proof points, differentiators, countries, price level, named customers, job titles seen in testimonials, tone of voice, and a source link on every fact.The overview screen and the email writer
Buyer groupsTwo or three groups. Each has a human half (name, why they buy, pain points) and a search half (titles, seniorities, company sizes, countries, exclusions).The screen, and Apollo
KeywordsTwo to five words per group describing the kind of company to search: wholesale, ecommerce, pharmacy.Apollo, to decide which companies

Two rules that keep it reliable

  • The model drafts, the code decides. Nothing the model returns reaches Apollo unchecked. Seniorities and size ranges must come from fixed lists, country names are normalised, job titles are trimmed and de-duplicated, the customer's own domain is forced into the exclusion list, and any fact without a source is dropped.
  • Unknown stays unknown. If the site does not say it, the field is empty and confidence drops. A low confidence run shows the three questions instead of a confident wrong answer.

Keywords decide the companies, titles decide the people. "Founder" exists in every industry, so without keywords a search returns founders of schools and restaurants. And because keywords are OR'd together, every extra keyword makes the audience wider, not narrower.

How the data reaches Apollo

Each buyer group's search half becomes one HTTP call. The field names in our analysis output were chosen to map one to one onto Apollo's parameters, which is why the schema looks the way it does.

Our fieldApollo parameterEffect
titlesperson_titles[]which people
senioritiesperson_seniorities[]how senior
keywordsq_organization_keyword_tags[]which companies
company_sizesorganization_num_employees_ranges[]how big, as "11,50"
countriesorganization_locations[]where
exclude_domainsnot_organization_websites_list[]never themselves or a rival
email_statuscontact_email_status[]only people we can actually reach

The call, and why the preview is free

Sizing uses Apollo's people search endpoint, /api/v1/mixed_people/api_search. It costs no credits and it returns no email addresses, only a masked last name and a flag saying an email exists. That is exactly why the free preview can be generous without leaking data or spending money.

The cache key is a hash of the enabled groups and their filters, so editing a group re-sizes it, while switching back to a previous combination costs nothing. Paying for people happens later and separately, through Apollo's bulk enrichment, and only for leads a live campaign actually needs.

What runs after launch

Once a campaign is live, the product becomes a set of scheduled jobs. Cloud Scheduler calls the API, the API runs the job, and Redis locks stop two runs overlapping.

JobWhat it doesStatus
sourcingApollo search, enrich the new people, drop duplicates and anyone on the blocklist, score them, put them into the week's batch, then hand the batch to a human for review and post a Slack notice.core loop ported
sendSends today's approved emails through Smartlead, inside the daily caps.working
reply-syncSweeps for replies and classifies them, and keeps follow-ups in order.webhooks are the source of truth
auto-replyDrafts and sends an answer where the rules allow it, with a gate that hands anything sensitive to a human.working
blocklist, reverifyKeeps the do-not-contact list current and re-checks aging email addresses.working
billing-sync, reportKeeps Stripe state in step and sends the summary.working

What the customer sees on top of that is the cockpit (today's sends, what needs them, this week against last), the inbox with replies sorted by what they mean, the lead list with filters and CSV in and out, campaign controls, and one emergency stop that pauses everything at once.

The database

One Prisma schema, 781 lines, about thirty models. They group into five families:

  • Identity: User, EmailChallenge, OAuthAccount, RefreshToken, Organization, Membership.
  • Money: Plan, Subscription, Payment, Refund, Invoice, WebhookEvent, UsageCounter.
  • Setup: Onboarding, SendingDomain, Mailbox, OrgSettings, IntegrationCredential.
  • Outreach: Campaign, Company, Contact, Message, WeeklyBatch, BlocklistEntry, AutoReplyQueue, AutoReplyDecision.
  • Operations: SystemLog, and the status enums that keep states honest, such as a sending domain being pending registration.

The onboarding state is not spread across tables. It is one Onboarding row per organisation holding the domain, the facts, the buyer groups, the cached market figures, the preview and the analysis errors.

The sizing rules live in components/onboarding/sizing.ts on the web side and in config/plans.ts on the API side, so a change to sends-per-inbox touches two files and a test.

Tests

Nineteen API test files and eleven on the web. The two worth knowing about are tenant-isolation.test.ts, which proves one organisation cannot read another's rows, and onboarding.test.ts plus analysis.test.ts, which pin the analysis output and the repair rules so a prompt change cannot quietly break the Apollo search.

Five conventions to follow when adding anything. Controllers validate and delegate, never contain logic. Every query is scoped to the organisation. Vendors are only reached through the integrations folder. Anything that takes more than a second becomes a job. Secrets come from the environment and Secret Manager, never from code.

What works, and what does not yet

This is the honest version, taken from the feature map and the migration plan in the repository, not from what the screens imply.

Working end to end

  • Registration, sign in, Google sign-in, teams and roles, invitations.
  • The analysis, including the fallback when a site cannot be read, and the editable facts and groups.
  • Market sizing and sample prospects from Apollo, the sample email sequence, the plan recommendation.
  • Stripe checkout, subscriptions, invoices, the customer billing page.
  • Campaign creation and provisioning in Smartlead, the cockpit, the inbox, leads, blocklist, settings, the admin area.

Partly working

PieceWhat is missingWhat the app does today
Sending domainsNo registrar integration, so nothing is actually boughtIdeas are generated and checked against DNS, the customer chooses and pays, and the domain sits as pending registration
InboxesNo provider that creates Google or Microsoft mailboxesPlanned inboxes are stored as planned, and real sending uses mailboxes already connected in Smartlead
Sourcing jobSome heuristics from the source app were specific to one customer and were left outThe main loop runs: search, enrich, dedupe, score, batch

The three things that block the promise

  1. Buy the domain. Until a registrar API is wired in, "we buy your domains" is not true. This is one integration file.
  2. Create the inbox. Same shape of problem. A mailbox vendor closes it, and pre-warmed mailboxes would also remove the two week wait.
  3. Price the inbox above cost. Mailboxes cost roughly four and a half dollars a month from a vendor. Selling them at four loses money on every one.

One more to settle before launch. Apollo's API terms license the data for internal business purposes and say you may not sell or sublicense access to a third party. Our model shows Apollo data to our customers, so it needs written clarification from Apollo. The code already keeps every provider behind an interface, so swapping the data source is a contained change rather than a rewrite.

How this differs from AutoGTM

AutoGTM, by Explee, is the closest thing to this product on the market. Both start from a website address. The difference is who owns the sending.

AutoGTMAperture
Who sendsTheir own pool of pre-warmed inboxesThe customer's own new domains and inboxes
Time to first emailMinutesTwo weeks of warmup, or days on pre-warmed inboxes
Who owns the domainsThey doThe customer keeps them
Where replies landInside their platformThe customer's own inbox and ours
DataTheir own database with semantic searchApollo filters plus MillionVerifier
Free previewFull names and real email addressesMasked names, no addresses before payment
PriceThree cents per email sent, no subscriptionPlan plus inboxes and domains, monthly
To startThirty dollars in credits, card not chargedA few hundred dollars up front

Where they are stronger: starting today, and a very small first commitment. Where we are stronger: the customer sends from their own brand, keeps the domains as an asset, and never shares deliverability with strangers. The obvious move is to offer both, an instant start on pre-warmed inboxes and the owned setup, rather than pretending the two week wait is a feature.

Their presentation is worth copying even though their architecture is not: six named stages ticking off one by one, each finished stage docking into the left rail, and a live log of what the agent is doing. Same work, and it feels like research instead of a loading screen.

10

Questions people ask

Why not just ask the customer who their buyers are?

Most cannot answer it in the form Apollo needs, and a long form at signup loses most of the people who started. Their website already contains the answer, so we read it and ask them to confirm instead.

What if the model invents the audience?

Four guards. Every fact carries the page it came from and unsourced facts are dropped. Seniorities and company sizes must come from fixed lists. The customer edits the groups before anything runs. And a wrong audience shows up immediately as a strange number in the free count.

Why is the preview free if Apollo costs money?

Because the search endpoint we use for sizing costs zero credits and returns no email addresses. We only pay for people once a paying campaign needs to contact them.

Why can't the campaign start the same day?

Brand new mailboxes have no sending reputation. Sending at full volume from them lands in spam and can poison the domain. Fourteen days of warmup is the standard advice, and pre-warmed inboxes are the way to skip it.

Why separate domains instead of the customer's real one?

Cold email carries reputation risk. If a campaign trips a spam filter, that should happen on a spare domain, not on the one their invoices and customer emails depend on. The spare domains forward visitors to the real site.

What stops us emailing the same person twice, or someone who said no?

A unique index on organization plus email, a suppression list that every batch is checked against, and per-company caps. Unsubscribes and bounces go straight onto that list.

Why are the long jobs not just API calls?

The analysis takes up to a minute and provisioning takes hours. Request timeouts make that fragile, so anything slow runs as a job with locks and retries, and the screen follows progress separately.

What happens if a customer's site is blocked or empty?

Fallbacks in order: plain fetch, then a rendered fetch, then web search, then three questions. The confidence score decides when to stop trusting the site and ask.

Can we swap Apollo for something else?

Yes. Every provider sits behind an interface in the integrations folder, so a replacement is a new file, not a rewrite. That matters because of the open terms question.

What would I build next, in order?

Registrar integration, then an inbox provider with pre-warmed mailboxes, then the inbox price fix, then the live progress stream so the onboarding shows its work.
